Experiment Purpose

This study aims to answer a core question for income-oriented investors holding QQQ:

  • Primary Goal: Find the optimal (DTE, Delta, Profit Taking, Stop Loss) combination that maximizes the Sharpe Ratio (risk-adjusted return) while keeping the "called away" frequency low — preserving long-term equity upside.
  • Secondary Goal: Compare the after-tax total return of a DIY covered call strategy on QQQ versus buying QQQI (NEOS QQQ High Income ETF), which implements a similar covered call overlay but with Section 1256 tax treatment on its distributions.
  • Tertiary Goal: Quantify the tax drag of covered call premiums taxed as ordinary income (37%) vs Section 1256 treatment (60/40 rule, ~29.1% blended), and evaluate whether the tax structure of QQQI justifies its management fee and potential tracking error.
  • Practical Question: In a predominantly bullish market regime (2021–2025), does writing covered calls add value or does the capped upside offset the premium income? Under what parameter combinations does CC outperform pure Buy & Hold?

4. Option Pricing Model

Options are priced daily using the Black-Scholes model with:
• Implied Volatility: Calibrated from CBOE VIX (×1.05 scaling for QQQ beta) with real-time daily updates
• Volatility Skew: Applied as σadj = σbase + 0.12 × σbase × (1 − K/S) for OTM calls
• Risk-Free Rate: 4% (constant, approximate Fed Funds 2021–2025 average)
• Strike Selection: Analytic inversion of BS delta formula, rounded to realistic listed strike intervals
